Before you continue
Taking over a lease is a legal commitment. Please read and acknowledge each of the following — tap each card to confirm you understand.
📋
Lease assignment requires landlord consent
You cannot simply take over a lease without the landlord's written approval. Both parties must agree in writing and the landlord must authorise the transfer before you move in or make any payment.
Consumer Affairs Victoria — Assignment & subletting →
🏠
Always inspect the property first
Never pay a bond, deposit, or any money without physically inspecting the property in person. Scammers often create urgency to pressure payment before inspection. If you cannot inspect in person, arrange a trusted representative.
CAV — Inspecting a rental property →
📄
Entry condition report is mandatory
Within 3 business days of moving in, you must complete and return a signed entry condition report documenting the property's condition. This protects your bond when you leave. Under the Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (Vic) this is a legal requirement.
CAV — Condition reports →
💰
Bond must be lodged with the RTBA
In Victoria, bonds must be lodged with the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA) — not kept by the landlord. If anyone asks you to pay bond directly to them and not through the RTBA, this is illegal.
RTBA Victoria — Bond lodgement →
📜
Read the full lease before signing
You are taking on all existing lease terms and conditions. Understand the break lease fees, notice periods, rent review clauses, and pet/modification policies before committing. Request a full copy of the lease from the current tenant.
CAV — Rental agreements explained →
⚖️
Know your renter rights under Victorian law
Victoria's Residential Tenancies Act 1997 gives renters strong protections — including minimum standards, repairs, privacy, and anti-discrimination rights. VCAT (Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal) handles disputes.
CAV — Renters rights & responsibilities →
⚠️
Critical: You may be inheriting an existing bond & condition report — and all the risk that comes with it
This is one of the most common ways students lose their entire bond.

When you take over an existing lease, the property's entry condition report was completed at the start of the original tenancy — not when you moved in. At the end of the tenancy, the property must be returned to the condition described in that original report, regardless of what it looked like when you arrived.

The risk: If the vacating tenant left damage, excessive wear, or uncleanliness that isn't in the original report, you may be held liable for it on exit — even though it wasn't your fault.

Protect yourself — before any money changes hands:
  • → Sit down with the vacating tenant and review the original entry condition report together, room by room
  • → Photograph and video every room, every wall, every appliance — before you sign anything
  • → Note any differences between the original report and the current condition in writing
  • → Request the vacating tenant arranges a professional clean at their cost before handover
  • → Do not trust a stranger's word — get everything documented and signed
  • → Never transfer bond money until you are completely satisfied with the property's documented condition
CAV — Understanding condition reports →
International students: UniRenter strongly recommends reviewing the Consumer Affairs Victoria renting guide, contacting Tenants Victoria for free legal advice, and checking VCAT if you have a dispute.

Victorian renter rights & resources
🏛️
Consumer Affairs Victoria (CAV)
The primary Victorian government authority for rental law. Comprehensive guides on bonds, repairs, inspections, disputes, and renter rights under the Residential Tenancies Act 1997.
consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting →
🏦
Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA)
All bonds in Victoria must be lodged here — not with landlords. Use this site to lodge, track, and claim your bond. International students: never pay bond directly to a landlord or agent without RTBA lodgement.
rtba.vic.gov.au →
⚖️
Tenants Victoria — Free Legal Advice
Free, independent legal advice for Victorian renters. Call their advice line or use their online resources. Particularly recommended for international students unfamiliar with Australian tenancy law.
tenantsvic.org.au · Advice line: 03 9416 2577 →
🏛️
VCAT — Residential Tenancies List
The Victorian Civil and Administrative Tribunal handles rental disputes — bond claims, repairs, eviction challenges, and more. Filing a VCAT application is free or low-cost for renters.
vcat.vic.gov.au →
📚
Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (Vic)
The full text of the legislation that governs all rental agreements in Victoria. Useful for understanding your exact legal rights and obligations as a renter or lease transferee.
legislation.vic.gov.au →
🌏
Study Victoria — International Student Housing Guide
Victorian government guide specifically for international students, covering accommodation types, scam awareness, renter rights, and support services in Victoria.
study.vic.gov.au →
🚩 Before you share details or arrange a meetup
Take 60 seconds to check these red flags. Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, it probably is.
💸
Asked for payment before inspection

Legitimate landlords and tenants never ask for bond or rent before you have physically inspected the property.

📱
Refuses a video call or in-person meeting

If someone won't do a video call or meet in person, assume the listing is fake.

💰
Rent seems significantly below market rate

If it seems too good to be true, it is. Research comparable rooms before engaging.

⏰
Creating urgency — "3 other people want it"

Scammers manufacture pressure. A legitimate person will give you time to inspect and decide.

🏦
Bond not going through RTBA

All Victorian bonds must be lodged with the RTBA — never paid directly to anyone.
